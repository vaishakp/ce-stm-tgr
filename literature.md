## 6. What can GW observations say, if any, about QG?
### Authors: Elias Vagenas, ...

It is widely recognized that classical general relativity (GR) is an incomplete and effective theory resulting from a more fundamental yet-to-be-known theory of quantum gravity. The central issue is the appearance of spacetime singularities in GR. Other open problems, such as the dark matter and dark energy problems, also suggest that the structure of spacetime itself may hold the key to a deeper understanding, reinforcing the need for a more fundamental theory from which GR can emerge as a low-energy limit. While numerous quantum gravity candidates, both fundamental and phenomenological, have been proposed, a major challenge remains: the lack of experimental evidence. However, with the advent of new experimental capabilities, particularly through gravitational wave (GW) observatories, we are now positioned to collect data that could offer insights into the nature of quantum gravity and the fundamental fabric of spacetime. In particular, observations of compact binary mergers, black holes, and other high-energy astrophysical phenomena could reveal quantum gravity signatures hidden within GWs, such as modified waveforms (amplitude, frequency, dispersion relations), quasinormal modes, GW echoes, and beyond-tensor polarizations. Therefore, we must take advantage of these new experimental opportunities to test quantum gravity theories, moving them beyond speculative conjectures and into the realm of experiments.
